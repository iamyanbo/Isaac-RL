# Independent Isaac RL audit — 2026-09-13

## Bottom line

The primary bottleneck is **task/data coverage plus an objective that is not causal for control**, with policy confidence/credit assignment as secondary problems. The PPO implementation is running and numerically healthy; GPU/CPU optimization is not the limiting factor. Kills and damage are real native events after the HP-delta fix, but they are not evidence that the policy aimed, moved, or dodged to cause them.

The six-worker learner was live during this audit (PID 51580, session `session-1789334365873010900`, CPU, instances 0/1/2/4/5/6). The last check was 1,616,232 steps / 2,386 updates / 5,018 episodes, six established trainer-owned sockets, empty learner stderr, and 0 recent wins. This report did not stop, signal, or attach a second client to the trainer.

It contains 16,858 action transitions: `move=0` on 52.5%, `shoot!=0` on 71.9%. In combat states, `move=0` on 53.6% while `shoot!=0` on 99.9%; consecutive state samples have zero x/y displacement on 85.9% of all transitions (38.7%/59.2% zero velocity components in combat). Native kill callbacks and HP deltas are valid event accounting.

## Bottleneck assessment

**Reward design / observability: high risk.** `confirmed_v3` pays +0.2 per resolved damage, +0.5 per kill, +10 per combat clear, +2 per newly visited room, and +100 only for a verified boss clear; it also pays only -0.01 per transition and -0.5 per damage taken. Those terms make passive damage/room clearing locally profitable while the boss outcome is rarely sampled. The reward is outcome-based but not action-causal: it cannot distinguish deliberate aim/dodge from an enemy walking into a standing shooter. Per-step reward components are not retained in production JSONL.

**Data / horizon: highest-confidence failure.** Only ~1.4% of episodes reach a boss, and most episodes die or idle after ~2–3 rooms. The learner therefore receives almost no boss combat transitions and no successful terminal target to imitate or bootstrap. Six workers increase samples per wall-clock hour but do not change this distribution or make the boss signal less sparse.

**Optimization: secondary, not broken.** PPO loss, gradients, KL, and critic EV are finite. The actor's entropy remains close to the maximum and policy loss is small, so a high entropy coefficient (0.02) may be preventing commitment. Lowering entropy alone is unsafe: with this data distribution it can collapse into a confident passive policy.

**Architecture/control: secondary risk.** The policy is feed-forward and decisions are held for eight native frames. Observations include current enemy positions/velocities and a visited-room summary, but no action history or explicit target/trajectory representation. Fine dodging and directional aim are consequently difficult, especially when the policy already spends half of combat transitions with no movement.

**Infrastructure: not the learning bottleneck.** Six workers are connected, the learner is advancing, CPU collection is the dominant measured cost, and the localhost charts/lifecycle service is read-only. No current evidence indicates a bridge stall or corrupted checkpoint.

## What is missing

The production trainer logs terminal episode aggregates and per-update losses/timings, not per-transition action, player displacement, enemy-relative geometry, reward components, or action-conditioned damage. `live-states.json` is a latest snapshot, not a trajectory. Therefore the current run cannot answer directly whether movement, shooting, and dodging caused each kill. The 1M deterministic trace is a useful behavioral warning, not a current-run proof.

There is also no fresh held-out evaluation of the current 1.6M checkpoint. Training curves and shaped returns must not substitute for that evaluation.

## Highest-value next experiment

Do one **instrumented, fixed-checkpoint behavioral A/B** before changing the learner:

1. Evaluate the current checkpoint on at least 20 unseen normal seeds, deterministic and stochastic, with a trace containing every action head, player displacement/velocity, enemy-relative positions, resolved damage delta, kills, room transitions, damage taken, and terminal reason.
2. Run the same seeds with a no-movement/shoot-only ablation and a movement-enabled/shoot-enabled comparison. Report boss reach, combat-clear rate, damage per combat transition, damage taken, displacement, and success—not just shaped return.
3. If movement-enabled behavior does not improve damage taken or boss reach, create a separate experiment (do not overwrite this run) with a combat curriculum or replay/oversampling of combat and boss states, shorter action hold (2–4 frames), and a scheduled lower entropy coefficient (for example 0.02→0.005). Keep the current six-worker run/checkpoint as the control.

This experiment has the highest information value because it distinguishes passive-reward exploitation from an observation/control failure before spending another large training budget. Reward redesign should follow the trace: preserve resolved HP accounting, add only validated causal/behavioral terms, and ensure boss/combat data is deliberately represented. Do not declare progress from kills, damage, explained variance, or shaped returns alone.
