# Independent Isaac RL audit — 2026-09-13

## Finding and confidence

The strongest finding is **poor task performance and sparse boss coverage**. The causal learning bottleneck is not yet established. Reward incentives, exploration, control, and representation remain competing explanations. PPO runs with finite numerical metrics and little update-time overhead; that does not prove its learning implementation or hyperparameters are adequate. Resolved HP loss and native deaths are better event accounting than attempted hits, but neither establishes deliberate aim or dodging, or attributes damage exclusively to the player.

Follow-up correction: the original audit overstated a reward-design diagnosis. Outcome rewards need not explicitly measure deliberate action to support learning, and standing still can be a good combat strategy in some states. Moderate critic explained variance is measured against shaped return targets and says nothing directly about control competence. No current-policy passive-shooting rate has yet been measured with a representative trajectory. The paired diagnostic below is now implemented to resolve part of that uncertainty.

The six-worker learner was live during this audit (PID 51580, session `session-1789334365873010900`, CPU, instances 0/1/2/4/5/6). The last check was 1,616,232 steps / 2,386 updates / 5,018 episodes, six established trainer-owned sockets, empty learner stderr, and 0 recent wins. This report did not stop, signal, or attach a second client to the trainer.

Across the recorded run (1,939 episodes at the audit snapshot), there were 25 boss sightings and zero wins. Returns vary but do not establish a monotonic competence trend; the apparent late reward bumps coincide with variance in episode length, clears, and kills rather than a sustained rise in boss reach or success.

The closest direct behavior trace is the deterministic held-out evaluation of the ~1M-step checkpoint, not the current live policy. It contains 16,858 action transitions: `move=0` on 52.5%, `shoot!=0` on 71.9%. In combat states, `move=0` on 53.6% while `shoot!=0` on 99.9%; consecutive state samples have zero x/y displacement on 85.9% of all transitions (38.7%/59.2% zero velocity components in combat). Nonzero movement transitions have more displacement and more damage transitions than `move=0`. This is consistent with “stand and shoot / enemies enter tears,” but cannot be promoted to a claim about every current six-worker trajectory because production does not trace actions.

The corrected bridge does measure resolved enemy HP loss (`hp_delta_v1`) and separately retains diagnostic attempted damage. Current episode logs have `damage_attempted` and `damage_dealt`; their closeness is evidence that the old invulnerable-hit inflation was fixed, not evidence of active aiming. Native kill callbacks and HP deltas are valid event accounting.

## Bottleneck assessment

**Reward design / observability: plausible contributor.** `confirmed_v3` pays +0.2 per resolved damage, +0.5 per kill, +10 per combat clear, +2 per newly visited room, +100 for a verified boss clear, -0.01 per transition, -0.5 per damage-taken event unit, and -5 on death. These terms can reward local damage and clears even on failed runs. That is a hypothesis about incentives, not proof of exploitation or a requirement to reward motion. HP changes and deaths are not attributed to a tear owner; environmental damage and NPC behavior may contribute. Per-step reward components are not retained in production JSONL. The recorded `clears` count includes empty rooms; observed `combat_clears` is the more relevant but still imperfect measure.

**Data / horizon: highest-confidence failure.** Only ~1.4% of episodes reach a boss, and most episodes die or idle after ~2–3 rooms. The learner therefore receives almost no boss combat transitions and no successful terminal target to imitate or bootstrap. Six workers increase samples per wall-clock hour but do not change this distribution or make the boss signal less sparse.

**Optimization: unresolved.** Finite loss, gradients, KL, and critic EV rule out obvious numerical failure. They do not rule out a learning bug, poor gradient balance, or unsuitable hyperparameters. Total entropy combines movement, shooting, and utility; invalid/unavailable utility actions are not masked and can contribute entropy without useful exploration. Small PPO surrogate loss is not a competence test. A lower entropy coefficient is a candidate controlled experiment after per-head and behavioral measurements, not an established fix.

**Architecture/control: plausible contributor.** The policy is feed-forward and decisions are held for eight native updates. Observations include enemy/projectile velocities, terrain, and visitation features, but lack action history and several combat details. Whether recurrence, an entity encoder, or a shorter action duration would improve control has not been tested. The measured ~54% no-movement combat rate belongs to the older deterministic checkpoint, not the current stochastic learner.

**Infrastructure: not the learning bottleneck.** Six workers are connected, the learner is advancing, CPU collection is the dominant measured cost, and the localhost charts/lifecycle service is read-only. No current evidence indicates a bridge stall or corrupted checkpoint.

## What is missing

The production trainer logs terminal episode aggregates and per-update losses/timings, not per-transition action, player displacement, enemy-relative geometry, reward components, or action-conditioned damage. `live-states.json` is a latest snapshot, not a trajectory. Therefore the current run cannot answer directly whether movement, shooting, and dodging caused each kill. The 1M deterministic trace is a useful behavioral warning, not a current-run proof.

There is also no fresh held-out evaluation of the current 1.6M checkpoint. Training curves and shaped returns must not substitute for that evaluation.

## Highest-value next experiment

The separate `isaac_rl.behavior_eval` diagnostic now implements an instrumented, fixed-checkpoint comparison:

1. Freeze one current checkpoint. On 20 normal seeds unseen by that checkpoint, evaluate deterministic and stochastic policies plus a stochastic arm with movement suppressed only while enemies are present. All other action heads retain the policy output. Disabling movement from spawn would confound combat with failure to navigate out of the initial room.
2. Use the same per-seed action RNG seed for both stochastic arms, rotate arm order, and compare hashes of the complete observation prefix through first combat entry. Exclude unmatched prefixes and cases that never reach combat from the movement comparison. Record one stochastic repetition per seed as a limitation.
3. Retain full native traces plus per-step proposed/executed actions, per-head probabilities and entropy, displacement (excluding room-transition teleports), raw reward components, event deltas, and terminal states. Compare combat clears, damage taken, boss reach and wins. Damage during stationary transitions is an association; existing tears, environmental hazards and NPC behavior prevent assigning it causally to that immediate action.
4. After those results, choose one training intervention at a time. An entropy schedule, shorter action hold, or fresh on-policy combat curriculum are candidates. Reusing old transitions as ordinary PPO rollout data is not an appropriate way to oversample combat; any curriculum must collect fresh data and preserve a separate full-floor evaluation. Changing all three factors at once would obscure which factor helped.

This experiment measures how the current frozen policy uses movement and whether movement suppression changes outcomes on comparable seeds. It will not by itself uniquely identify reward, architecture, or optimization as the cause of the plateau. In particular, do not add movement rewards merely because stationary behavior looks undesirable. The success criterion remains consistent full first-floor boss clears on unseen seeds.
