# Isaac RL follow-up audit — September 14, 2026

## Decision

The policy still does not reliably navigate and survive early rooms. Boss experience remains scarce, and there are **zero recorded wins** in the corrected training window through 2.374M steps. This is a demonstrated task-performance failure, not yet a uniquely identified reward, optimization, or architecture defect.

The completed behavior evaluation weakens the hypothesis that the stochastic learner's main problem is standing still. Its frozen 1.630M policy selected no movement on 17.7% of combat actions and had less than one unit of same-room net displacement on **8.85%**. The deterministic policy behaved very differently: 56.3% stationary combat transitions, 15/20 idle endings, and only nine seeds reaching combat. Do not generalize deterministic footage to stochastic training or equate movement with useful dodging.

The highest-value next experiment is a **frozen learned-policy versus uniform-random control**, with repeated native rollouts. It is now running beside the untouched six-worker learner. Before changing rewards or architecture, establish whether the policy's room clears and damage offer a meaningful advantage over chance.

For the other 13, the first transition-level difference was policy probabilities while player state and proposed actions still matched. The corresponding native states differed only in entities: tear or bomb geometry in 12 cases, enemy geometry in one. Native randomness not controlled by the game seed/action RNG is consistent with this evidence; the exact underlying RNG source has not been established. Do not relax the matching threshold after seeing outcomes.

Within the seven eligible seeds, normal minus suppressed movement averages +15.14 damage, +0.286 combat clears, and +0.571 damage-taken event units. Two seeds improved combat clears and five tied; all seven tied at zero wins. Movement suppression therefore does not explain away all recorded combat progress, but this small selected subset cannot establish a general effect or useful dodging. Even matched observations do not guarantee matched hidden RNG.

The stochastic policy's step-weighted movement/shooting/utility entropies are 1.991/1.429/1.240 nats; all three heads remain diffuse. Utility is not the sole source of high entropy. There were 694 bomb commands with zero bombs across 6,742 stochastic transitions. This proves wasted commands exist, not that masking alone will solve learning. Full utility availability is not represented in the current observation.

Health accounting was cross-checked: 121 sampled HP-loss units, five HP-gain units, and 122 damage-taken event units in the stochastic arm. Four transitions disagree between event count and net sampled HP delta. Keep these measures separate; pickups and multiple events inside an eight-update action can obscure net effects.

## Bottleneck assessment and missing evidence

- **Data and control:** strong evidence of weak early-room performance and sparse boss exposure. This is the immediate obstacle to the full-floor objective, but may itself result from optimization, incentives, or representation.
- **Reward:** local damage/clear rewards permit positive returns on failed runs. Exploitation or a need for movement rewards is unproven. Do not reward motion just to change how gameplay looks.
- **Optimization:** high per-head entropy and deterministic idling motivate a controlled entropy experiment, but finite loss/KL do not certify PPO correctness. There is no learning-rate, entropy-coefficient, actor/critic-gradient, or small-task learnability comparison yet.
- **Architecture:** feed-forward state encoding and eight-update action holds remain plausible limitations. No matched architecture or action-duration experiment isolates them.
- **Observability:** the old stationary-rate claim was checkpoint/mode-specific. The new traces repair that evidence gap, but do not provide action-source damage attribution or a direct dodging measure. The 1.630M evaluation is not a measurement of the now-2.37M policy.
- **Infrastructure:** the learner is live and advancing; collection dominates time. More GPU update capacity is not an explanation for the competence plateau.

## Next experiment, launched without changing training

`runs/baseline-20260914` freezes checkpoint **2,372,664**, SHA256 `17a91da77688e2e74aedc1feea058c77b10ec94ac127d5a627c08fc49a0898bf`.

Twenty seeds unseen by that frozen checkpoint, three repetitions per seed, two arms: learned stochastic and uniform random, totaling 120 episodes. Both use the same normal first-floor environment, character, 9/5/4 action heads, eight-update action duration, 3,375-action cap, and 900-action idle limit. Uniform random uses no controller, demonstrations, action mask, or privileged targets. Arm order is counterbalanced across seeds/repetitions. Native RNG can diverge; this is a repeated seed-blocked comparison, not an exact state-replay counterfactual.

Primary diagnostic comparison: combat clears per episode, averaged within seed before comparing arms. Report the 20 seed-level differences and uncertainty; do not treat 60 repeated episodes as 60 independent seeds. Also report full-floor wins, boss reach, death/idle distribution, sampled HP loss, damage, and action/displacement behavior. Shaped return is secondary. A clear damage advantage without better clears/survival is not enough. An inconclusive difference is not proof of equivalence to random.

If useful control is clearly above random but full-floor coverage remains poor, the next training experiment should change only the initial-state distribution: a separate, fresh on-policy combat curriculum versus a same-checkpoint full-floor control, with equal transition budgets and unchanged PPO/reward/architecture. Preserve normal unseen full-floor evaluation and never mix arbitrary old replay into vanilla PPO. If even local performance is indistinguishable with adequate precision, first test learnability on a small controlled room set before committing more full-floor hours; inspect observation/action alignment and gradient contributions there. Do not simultaneously change entropy, architecture, and rewards.
